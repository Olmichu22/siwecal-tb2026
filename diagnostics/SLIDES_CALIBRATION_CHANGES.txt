 SiW-ECAL TB2026 -- WHAT CHANGED IN THE CALIBRATION, AND HOW TO DEFEND IT
 A guide to the slides: the story, the plots, and the things NOT to claim.
 Written 2026-07-14. Companion to CALIBRATION_PROCEDURE.txt (the how).
================================================================================

THE STORY IN THREE SENTENCES
----------------------------
1. The low gain cannot be calibrated on its own MIP -- the MIP is buried in the
   noise -- so we stopped trying, and anchored it to the high gain instead.
2. The high-gain MIP measured at th230 is inflated 1.47x, not because the gain
   differs but because the trigger eats the peak, so every run is now calibrated
   against th220's MIP table.
3. Together these move the absolute energy scale of run_000012 by +52%. They do not
   improve the resolution, and we should not pretend otherwise.


================================================================================
 CHANGE 1 -- MIP TABLES ALWAYS FROM th220, WHATEVER THRESHOLD THE RUN USED
================================================================================
WHAT WAS WRONG
  A run taken at th230 was calibrated with th230's MIP table. Sounds right. It is
  not: the fitted MIP MPV is 32.40 ADC at th230 and 21.69 at th220, a factor 1.47,
  for electronics that are IDENTICAL (FeedbackCap 15, HoldDelay 110, FSPeakTime 2
  in both run_000060 and run_000004 -- only ThresholdDAC differs). A MIP deposits
  the same charge either way, so it must give the same ADC. The discrepancy is the
  measurement, not the detector.

WHY
  The MIP histogram only fills when the channel FIRES. The discriminator therefore
  cuts the spectrum from the LEFT. At th230 the cut lands at 18-22 ADC -- on top of
  the MIP peak itself (~22 ADC) -- and removes 70-83% of the peak-region entries.
  The Landau fit, seeing only the right-hand side of a peak, lands high.
  At th220 the cut is at 11-13 ADC, well clear of the peak, and the fit is clean:
  it agrees with the reference tool to 1.6%.

  ==> th230's MIP table is not correct even FOR th230.

  ### PLOT 1  (THE key plot -- this is the one that proves it)
      diagnostics/compare/th220_vs_th230_slab11_split30.png
      script: plot_th220_vs_th230.py

      The two MIP spectra, normalised on their TAILS (ADC > 40) -- a region no
      trigger reaches, so if the gains were equal they MUST overlap there.
      THEY DO (ratio 1.12; it would be 1.49 if the gains really differed).
      What does NOT overlap is the peak region: th230 is simply MISSING the entries
      its trigger never recorded.

      SAY: "Same gain, proven on the tails. The difference is entirely the
            entries the higher trigger threshold removed."
      This single plot carries the whole argument. If you show one, show this.

WHAT WE DID NOT CHANGE
  Pedestals stay on each run's OWN threshold. The trigger cannot bias a pedestal --
  it is measured from the samples that did NOT fire. And the th220/th230 pedestal
  tables genuinely differ (median 1.27 ADC in high gain, 68% of channel-SCAs apart
  by >1 ADC): that is real run-to-run drift, and forcing one onto the other would
  inject an error that does not exist today. Be ready for this question.


================================================================================
 CHANGE 2 -- THE LOW GAIN IS ANCHORED TO THE HIGH GAIN; MIP_lg IS GONE
================================================================================
WHAT WAS WRONG
  Above the saturation switch the hit energy was (adc_low - ped_lg) / MIP_lg.
  MIP_lg cannot be measured:

      a low-gain MIP = 2.08 ADC        (= k x MIP_hg)
      low-gain noise = 0.92-1.59 ADC
      -> S/N = 1.4-2.8, and in 14-23% of channels the NOISE EXCEEDS A WHOLE MIP.

  The Landau fit has nothing to grip and lands on 3.16-4.46 ADC -- inflated 1.5x to
  2x -- in EVERY ONE of the 7 muon runs, the quiet ones included. Note the sign:
  dividing by an inflated MIP UNDER-READS the energy of every saturated hit.

  ### PLOT 2  (why the low gain is hopeless on its own)
      diagnostics/th230/th230_mip_snr.png
      script: plot_mip_snr.py

      Four panels; the one that lands is the low-gain noise distribution with the
      MIP marked as a line THROUGH it. In the high gain the MIP stands at 11 sigma
      and you can see the peak with your eyes; in the low gain it is inside the
      noise.
      Also shows: the low-gain noise does NOT scale down with the gain. If it did it
      would be 1.95/10 = 0.19 ADC. It is 0.92 -- 5x more. The preamp's low-gain
      branch has its own noise floor. Dividing the signal by ten does not divide the
      noise by ten; it destroys the signal-to-noise.

      SAY: "We are not fitting this badly. There is nothing here to fit."

  ### PLOT 2b  (optional, if someone doubts the fits)
      diagnostics/th220/th220_lowgain_mip_fits.png
      script: plot_lowgain_mip_fits.py
      Actual low-gain Landau fits, channel by channel. Use only if challenged.

WHAT WE DO INSTEAD
  Anchor the low gain to the high gain, which IS well calibrated. In the FIDUCIAL
  REGION -- adc_high - pedestal in [150, 1200], below both the switch and the onset
  of non-linearity -- the two branches trace a straight line:

      adc_low - ped_lg  =  k (adc_high - ped_hg) + c

  Invert it to recover the high-gain ADC an unsaturated preamp would have given, and
  divide by MIP_hg:

      adc_high_equiv = (adc_low - ped_lg - c) / k
      energy         = adc_high_equiv / MIP_hg

  MIP_lg never appears.

  *** THIS IS STANDARD, NOT A FUDGE. *** It is dual-gain intercalibration: you
  determine the gain ratio in the overlap region where both branches are valid, and
  use the low gain to EXTEND THE DYNAMIC RANGE -- not as a second independent
  calibration. Say this explicitly on the slide; it is the obvious objection and the
  answer is that this is what everyone does.

  ### PLOT 3  (THE method slide)
      diagnostics/th220/gain_anchor_fit_000072_th220.png   (and th230/ for run12)
      script: plot_gain_anchor_fit.py

      Left panel: the adc_high/adc_low plane. GREEN SOLID = the fiducial region,
      the only place the line is measured. GREEN DASHED = the extrapolation, which
      is the only part actually USED. ORANGE = the switch. You can see the high gain
      bend over past it.
      Right panel: zoom on the pedestal point, where c is read off.

      Numbers to quote:
          k = 0.0962   (1/k = 10.4)   run12: 0.0961, run72: 0.0963  -- 0.2% apart
          c = 1.45 ADC

      k is measured in two runs at two DIFFERENT thresholds and agrees to 0.2%.
      That is the headline: the gain ratio is a property of the chip, and it
      reproduces.

      The plot also draws the LEAST-SQUARES line in red, and it visibly misses the
      dense part of the band (it reports c = +3.13 where the medians say +1.60).
      We trace the band with MEDIANS per slice, not least squares, because the tails
      drag a least-squares fit off the data. Mention this if you have room -- it is
      a nice methodological point and it pre-empts "why not just fit it?"


================================================================================
 CHANGE 3 -- THE SWITCH MOVES 1900 -> 1500
================================================================================
  Measured, not guessed. Use the LOW gain as the ruler (it is linear across this
  whole range; the high gain is the one that saturates), slice in adc_low, take the
  median adc_high per slice, and see where it falls below the straight line:

      raw adc_high     1200     1500     1700     1900
      non-linearity    0.4%     1.2%     1.9%     3.9%      (both runs agree)

  At the old switch the high gain was ALREADY 4% low, and every hit between 1500 and
  1900 carried that one-sided under-read. A bias does not average away over the hits
  of an event the way noise does.

  1900 existed to keep hits AWAY from the low gain, back when the low gain meant
  MIP_lg. That reason is gone.

  ### PLOT 4
      diagnostics/th230/hg_linearity_000012_th230.png
      script: plot_hg_linearity.py
      Right panel is the money panel: non-linearity in %, against raw adc_high, with
      the old and new switch marked.


================================================================================
 THE RESULT -- AND THE HONEST VERSION OF IT
================================================================================
  run_000012 (74 GeV electrons, th230), the same 108,624 events throughout:

      calibration                                    mu        sigma/mu
      ------------------------------------------------------------------
      OLD: th230 MIP + MIP_lg switch at 1900        2157      0.343
           th220 MIP + MIP_lg switch at 1900        3065      0.341
      NEW: th220 MIP + anchored LG, switch 1500     3290      0.350

  *** THE ABSOLUTE SCALE MOVES +52%. ***
  +42% from dropping th230's inflated MIP_hg, +7% from dropping MIP_lg on the
  saturated hits. Both were under-reads, so both push the same way.

  *** THE RESOLUTION DOES NOT IMPROVE. sigma/mu goes 0.343 -> 0.350, 2% WORSE. ***
  DO NOT HIDE THIS. Someone will ask. The answer is good:
    MIP_lg's inflation was close to a COMMON FACTOR across channels, so it was a
    SCALE error, not a scatter source -- and fixing a scale error moves the mean,
    not the width. Anchoring also raises the saturated hits ~50%, and those are the
    shower-core hits that fluctuate most, so sigma grows along with mu.
    WE GAINED SCALE, NOT RESOLUTION. Scale is what was broken.

  ### PLOT 5  (the result)
      diagnostics/th230/energy_calib_000012.png
      script: plot_energy_calib.py

      Old and new energy distributions on the SAME events. Panel 3 is the CONTROL:
      the 8,472 events with no saturated hit are bit-for-bit identical, proving
      nothing moved that should not have. Always show the control -- it is what
      makes this a measurement instead of a claim.


  ### THE STRONGEST SLIDE YOU HAVE, AND IT IS NOT THE MEAN
  ------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Move the switch from 1900 to 1500 and watch what each calibration does:

      anchored energy:    mu 3285 -> 3290    (+0.16%)
      old MIP_lg energy:  mu 3065 -> 2983    (-2.68%)

  A CORRECT intercalibration makes the two gain branches AGREE in their overlap
  region -- so it should not matter where you cross between them. It doesn't: 17x
  less sensitive than the old one.
  A BROKEN calibration makes your physics depend on an arbitrary tuning knob. That
  is exactly what MIP_lg was doing.

  This is the argument that does not depend on trusting any single number. Use it.


================================================================================
 WHAT NOT TO CLAIM  (read this before you write the slides)
================================================================================
* DO NOT claim the energy scale is now CORRECT. We know 3290 MIP is more correct
  than 2157 for 74 GeV. We do NOT have an absolute reference. The test is run_000072
  -- same 74 GeV beam, taken at th220, whose MIP was never biased. If run12 and run72
  converge on the same mu, the fix is validated. That reconstruction is running now.
  Until it lands: QUOTE THE CHANGE, NOT THE NUMBER.

* DO NOT claim the resolution improved. It did not. See above.

* DO NOT use the earlier run12-vs-run72 comparison (predicted mu ratio 1.469,
  measured 1.373). It was made with the OLD calibration and ~6.5% is unexplained.
  It is superseded.

* DO NOT quote the low-gain pedestal offset as a separate finding. The low-gain
  baseline really does sit ~1-2 ADC high on a channel that fired (measured directly,
  with a control that comes out at 0.00) -- but we do not know the mechanism, and I
  changed my mind about it three times in one afternoon. It is absorbed into c and
  that is where it should stay for now.

* DO NOT quote an error of +/-0.03 on c. That is what a least-squares covariance
  says and it is wrong. The real error is SYSTEMATIC, ~0.5 ADC: the value moves that
  much when you change the fit region. k is the solid one, not c.

* th210 IS BLOCKED, in case someone asks for a third threshold. 13 of its 14 runs
  decode to zero acquisitions -- the raw files are <run>.bin, not <run>_raw.bin, and
  the frame layout differs. It needs the format spec (Boudry/Taikan). The one th210
  run that does decode has FeedbackCap 8 -- a different gain -- so it cannot serve
  as the third point anyway.
