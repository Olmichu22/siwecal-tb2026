7. IN-SPILL vs OUT-OF-SPILL: THE EFFECT IS BEAM-INDUCED (2026-08-06)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Everything above is time-integrated and therefore silent on WHEN a cell's
baseline jumps. PedestalMipCalibrator gained a SpillSelect=all|in|out property
(cut on the acquisition's total accepted-hit occupancy) so the same fill can be
run twice over the same chunks, once on beam-on and once on beam-off
acquisitions. Study script: diagnostics/plot_pedestal_multipeak_spill.py.

THE SPLIT IS WELL DEFINED. Per-acquisition occupancy on run_000060 (th220) is
bimodal: median 4 hits when quiet, a second bump at 500-2000, and an almost
empty valley between ~50 and ~500 (0.3% of acquisitions per decade-bin there).
The high-occupancy acquisitions come in 11 contiguous blocks of ~40 separated by
long quiet stretches -- the SPS spill structure, not a rate fluctuation. 8.9% of
acquisitions are in-spill. Any cut in the valley (100/200/400) gives the same
answer; 800 does NOT -- it cuts inside the beam bump itself, so it is not a
spill cut. Default SpillOccupancyCut=200.

FIRST, A RESULT ABOUT THE PRODUCTION FILL. With the default NSlabsHit=8 the
comparison is impossible, and that is itself worth recording: the coincidence
tagger wants >=8 slabs lit at the same BCID, which IS a beam trigger. Of the
217,028 SCA slices it accepts over 10 chunks of run_000060, 99.14% come from
in-spill acquisitions and 0.86% from outside. Verified in the C++ too:
entries(in) + entries(out) = entries(all) exactly (13,566,256 + 117,263 =
13,683,519) with out at 0.857%. => Every pedestal in this repo's calibration is
an IN-SPILL pedestal. There is no out-of-spill sample to compare against unless
the coincidence is relaxed.

So both grids below were filled with NSlabsHit=1, so that ONLY the beam differs
between them. 59 chunks of run_000060, low gain:

                        cells present   entries      cells >=200
  in spill                  156,648    96,428,890       79,893
  out of spill               88,656     9,145,586        9,123

  RAW multi-peak fraction (each on its own cells, NOT comparable):
    in spill      16,305/79,893 = 20.41%
    out of spill     133/ 9,123 =  1.46%

STATISTICS-EQUALISED (intersection of cells, each resampled to exactly N --
the method of section 3, which is what makes the raw numbers above meaningless):

    N      common cells   SCA0 share    in spill   out of spill   ratio
   200         9,059         96.5%       20.74%        1.44%      14.5x
   500           687         81.7%       17.61%       10.77%       1.6x
  1000           252         75.0%       22.22%       16.27%       1.4x

  The N=200 row is stable across seeds (20.74/20.79/20.63 vs 1.44/1.58/1.49 for
  seeds 0/1/2), so the 14.5x is not a resampling fluctuation.

  DO NOT read the collapse to 1.4x at N=1000 as the effect disappearing under
  equalised statistics. The cell SET changes drastically down the rows: 9,059 ->
  252 cells. A cell only reaches 1000 out-of-spill entries if it self-triggers
  ~1000 times with no beam in the detector, i.e. only the pathologically noisy
  channels survive to the bottom row (s6_c11 alone contributes 316 of the 9,059
  and dominates the high-statistics tail). Those channels are multi-peak with or
  without beam -- overlaying them in/out gives two curves that sit on top of each
  other, same satellite comb. The bottom rows therefore isolate a small
  intrinsic, non-beam population; they do not describe the bulk.

THE RESULT THAT NEEDS NO PEAK-FINDING. Pedestal RMS on the same 9,059 cells,
paired cell by cell -- no TSpectrum, no threshold, no resampling:

    in spill      median 2.89 ADC   IQR [2.32, 3.47]
    out of spill  median 1.69 ADC   IQR [1.27, 2.33]
    widening      +1.05 ADC (x1.71), and 88.7% of cells are wider in spill

  A paired 88.7% is not a statistics artefact of any kind. The beam widens the
  baseline of essentially every cell.

TWO INDEPENDENT POPULATIONS, AND THEY ADD. The obvious objection to the "beam
does it" reading is that the cells called intrinsic might simply be the ones
where enough out-of-spill data happened to accumulate to SEE structure that is
there everywhere. It is not that. Binning the common cells by how much
out-of-spill data they hold and measuring BOTH sides at N=200 inside every bin
-- detection power constant across bins by construction, so any trend is a
property of the cells:

    out-of-spill      cells    multipeak @ N=200      median RMS (ADC)
    entries/cell               in spill   out          in     out
    [  200,   400)    7,565     21.20%     0.57%      2.88   1.63
    [  400,  1000)    1,052     19.20%     1.62%      3.03   2.19
    [ 1000,  4000)      189     22.22%     8.99%      2.39   1.73
    [ 4000,   inf)      253     21.34%    22.53%      2.08   1.91

  The in-spill column is FLAT at ~21% across all four bins: the beam-induced
  mechanism does not care how noisy a cell is intrinsically. The out-of-spill
  column climbs 0.57% -> 22.53%, at fixed detection power, so it is real.

  => Two mechanisms, independent and additive. The bulk (95% of cells) is
  multi-peak ONLY with beam. A small tail is multi-peak either way, and the
  beam adds its own ~21% on top. The N=500/N=1000 rows above are that tail seen
  in isolation, which is why their ratio collapses; it is not the effect going
  away.

  WHAT THAT TAIL ACTUALLY IS -- two whole chips, and NOT "noisy cells".
  Redone on the pure SCA0 set, the >=4000-entry group is 127 cells sitting on
  2 of the 139 chips in the sample: s13_c9 (all 64 channels) and s6_c11 (63).
  Whole chips, not scattered channels. That is expected once you notice the
  binning variable is a CHIP property: an SCA slice is recorded for all 64
  channels at once, so a cell's out-of-spill entry count measures how often its
  chip self-triggered with no beam, not how noisy that channel is. Measured:
  the within-chip spread of out-of-spill entry counts is 4.1% (median over the
  139 chips with >=10 cells).

  And "noisy" in the pedestal-WIDTH sense is the wrong picture -- it is a poor
  predictor, and it points the wrong way. At fixed detection power (N=200):

     by chip trigger rate            by the cell's own out-of-spill RMS
     (out-of-spill entries)
     [  200,  400)  7,502  0.47%     RMS [0.00, 1.26)  2,186  0.00%
     [  400, 1000)  1,052  2.00%     RMS [1.26, 1.67)  2,186  0.00%
     [ 1000, 4000)     63  1.59%     RMS [1.67, 2.29)  2,186  0.00%
     [ 4000,  inf)    127 17.32%     RMS [2.29,40.21)  2,186  3.61%

  The intrinsic group has the NARROWEST pedestals in the whole sample (median
  RMS 0.76 ADC, against 1.62 for the bulk). They are not wide, dirty baselines:
  they are very sharp peaks, several of them, at discrete levels. Whatever
  moves those two chips' baseline between levels does it cleanly and without
  beam.

NOT AN SCA-TO-SCA PEDESTAL DIFFERENCE -- now closed independently. The July
summary already argued this from the per-SCA plots (each individual SCA is
itself multi-peak, with the same 3 sub-peaks; only the WEIGHT among them
shifts). The spill split closes it a second way and more directly: 96.5% of the
cells compared here are SCA0, so it is one and the same SCA that is single-peak
without beam and a satellite comb with it. A pedestal offset between different
SCAs cannot produce that.

  Redone on a PURE SCA0 set (SCA=0, 100.0% SCA0, *_sca0.png) -- and the effect
  gets STRONGER, which also says the mixed numbers above are conservative:

      N      common cells    in spill   out of spill   ratio
     200         8,744        20.84%        0.78%      26.8x
     500           561        16.04%        5.88%       2.7x
    1000           189        19.58%       12.70%       1.5x

    RMS on the same 8,744 SCA0 cells: in 2.89, out 1.67 ADC, +1.09 (x1.73),
    88.8% of cells wider in spill.

  The mixed set reads 14.5x instead of 26.8x because the SCA1-4 sliver that
  survives out of spill carries multi-peak cells of its own -- consistent with
  the multi-peak fraction rising with SCA number (section 4).

CONSISTENT WITH THE GAIN ARGUMENT, NOT REDUNDANT WITH IT. The summary's HG/LG
result (offset ~equal in ADC in both gains => the step enters downstream of the
gain split) says the second population is NOT injected charge. This section says
it is nonetheless present only when beam is in the detector. Together: a
beam-CORRELATED disturbance entering downstream of the preamp -- common
mode / SCA memory / ADC chain reacting to detector activity, not signal leaking
into the pedestal sample. Neither result alone gets there.

SCOPE -- READ THIS BEFORE QUOTING THE 14.5x. Out of spill a chip is barely
triggered, so essentially only SCA0 fills: the N=200 common set is 96.5% SCA0.
These numbers are a statement about SCA0. Since the multi-peak fraction rises
with SCA number (section 4), they are a LOWER bound on the whole-detector
effect, not an average over it. Extending past SCA0 out of spill is not a
statistics problem that more chunks fix -- the higher SCAs are not written at
all when the chip is not triggered.

  Provenance: run_000060 only (59 chunks), th220, low gain. The other eight
  th220 runs are not in this and neither are th210/th230. The chunk-level
  numbers scale (1,377 chunks would give ~23x the out-of-spill statistics) but
  the SCA0 ceiling does not move.

9. THE BEAM ITSELF: IS SpillSelect REALLY CUTTING ON SPILLS? (2026-08-07)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
diagnostics/plot_beam_spill_structure.py, run over all 59 chunks of
run_000060. Acquisitions are spliced by acqNumber first (a chunk-boundary
acquisition is written twice as two complementary halves -- summing is the
splice; never dedup by position).

  acquisitions          28,860
  in spill (occ>200)     2,635  = 9.1% duty cycle
  spill blocks              29  median 101 consecutive acquisitions
  gaps                      30  median 1,062
  acquisition period      23 ms (acqWindowMs + delayBetweenCycleMs)
  => spill >~2.3 s, gap >~24.4 s, period >~26.7 s

  The seconds are LOWER BOUNDS: the config period leaves out readout dead time.
  The 9.1% duty cycle is a ratio of acquisition counts and is exact.

  The cut is sound: the occupancy distribution is cleanly bimodal with a wide
  empty valley, and the high-occupancy acquisitions come in 29 contiguous
  blocks, not scattered. The averaged spill profile is FLAT across the spill
  (no rise/fall), as slow extraction should be.

WHY THE OUT-OF-SPILL SAMPLE IS ALL SCA0 -- measured, not inferred.
  Filled SCA slots per triggered chip: median 3 in spill, median 1 out of it.
  With no beam a chip is triggered so rarely that it never gets past its first
  memory cell. This is the mechanism behind section 7's scope limitation, and
  it is why more chunks cannot fix it.

TRAP -- `bcid` WRAPS, USE `corrected_bcid`.
  The raw BCID is a 12-bit counter that rolls over every 4096 ticks, and an
  acquisition runs ~9 rollovers (corrected_bcid observed up to 38,569). Any
  time-structure plot built on raw bcid returns a mean of exactly 2048 = 4096/2
  for EVERY SCA slot -- a flat line that reads as "no time structure" and is
  pure artefact. With corrected_bcid (invalid sentinel -999) the mean rises
  linearly with slot number, ~1,600 BCID per slot in spill, which is a direct
  per-chip trigger-rate measurement. Out of spill it rises to slot ~3 and then
  flattens: the rare quiet acquisitions that fill deep slots are noise bursts,
  not slow accumulation.

  What is NOT retracted: the two-population structure itself (section 7's

  run_000254 is its own case: 8.30% of its cells are multi-peak out of spill,

  understood; it is the eudaq-converted run.

  invalid readings per cell, all in slices where the chip recorded nothing.

10c. CAN ANYTHING BE CORRECTED? (same script, both tests out of sample)

  RETRACTED -- "the ideal would be an occupancy-dependent pedestal" (the verbal
  conclusion carried since 2026-08-06). One offset per chip-activity bin, fitted
  on EVEN acquisitions and applied to ODD ones: RMS 4.133 -> 4.126, i.e. -0.2%,
  tail -0.5%. It buys nothing, and it could not have: what grows with occupancy
  is the WIDTH, and an offset only moves the mean.

  A per-slice common mode is the right shape of correction but only works in
  one regime. Estimator: the median deviation of the chip's OTHER quiet channels
  in the same slice (channels split even/odd, each half correcting the other, so
  never itself). Overall -4.0%, which hides everything:

    hits on the chip    RMS as calibrated -> corrected    removed
    [1,2)                     3.07 -> 1.66                46.0%
    [2,5)                     4.30 -> 4.30                -0.0%
    [5,10)                    4.27 -> 4.32                -1.1%
    [10,20)                   4.42 -> 4.25                 3.8%
    [20,50)                   4.70 -> 4.33                 7.8%
    [50,inf)                  7.37 -> 5.84                20.8%

  With the chip almost quiet the disturbance is ENTIRELY coherent: removing it
  gives 1.66 ADC against an out-of-spill pedestal RMS of 1.70 on the same cells.
  At 2-10 hits a chip-wide median removes nothing, so there the disturbance is
  not common to all 64 channels -- presumably localised near the struck
  channels. An estimator that excludes the neighbourhood of the hits is the
  obvious thing to try next; it has not been tried.

10d. THE ISOLATED OUT-OF-SPILL BURSTS ARE NOT A COUNTABLE POPULATION

  RETRACTED -- any rate or probability for the "isolated above-cut
  acquisitions" of section 9, including the estimate given verbally on
  2026-08-07. The train count is a property of the beam and is stable in the
  cut; the isolated count is not, and varies by more than an order of magnitude
  over cuts that are all defensible:

    run          trains at cut 25/50/100/200/400   isolated at the same cuts
    60              26 / 26 / 26 / 26 / 27            169 / 72 / 29 / 3 / 1
    86              28 / 28 / 28 / 28 / 28            213 / 45 /  8 / 1 / 0
    88             309 /309 /309 /309 /309            607 /397 /268 /173 /93
    62              24 / 19 / 17 / 16 / 15            661 /263 /162 /116 /95

  run_000060's three isolated acquisitions become 29 at cut 100 and 169 at cut
  25. They are the tail of a continuum, not a class with a rate. The three
  acquisitions characterised in section 9 remain real objects and their
  characterisation stands; what does not stand is counting them.

  THE CUT ITSELF took three attempts and the failures are worth recording,
  because all three are the same mistake in different clothes -- an absolute
  number tuned on one run:
    * fixed 200 hits (section 7's value): run_000142 and run_000143 came out
      with ZERO trains and 706 and 54 "bursts".
    * a valley finder on the log-occupancy histogram: returned 0-4 hits for
      every run, 92,067 bursts. At low occupancy the small integer counts leave
      exactly-empty log bins right next to the quiet mode and the argmin fell
      into one. Smoothed and floored, it returned cuts scattered 25-327 with no
      physical reason.
    * WHAT WORKS: anchor to the run's own beam level, defined as the median
      occupancy above its 99th percentile, and cut at 20% of it. On run_000060
      that gives 227 hits against the 200 chosen by eye in section 7, and
      reproduces its 26 trains and 3 isolated acquisitions exactly.

  IS THERE A SPILL AT ALL? A spill is contiguous, so in a run with beam almost
  every above-cut acquisition sits inside a train. Measured at each run's own
  cut: 98-100% for six runs, 75.6% for run_000062, against 5.6% for run_000142
  and 2.8% for run_000143. Those two have no beam -- their "beam level" is 122
  and 109 hits against 1,066-1,854 elsewhere -- and are excluded. This test is
  used in place of a floor in hits precisely because a floor in hits is what
  failed twice above.

  With that, the seven runs with beam hold 384,050 acquisitions, 533 spill
  trains and 340 isolated above-cut acquisitions. The train count is the number
  worth having; the 340 is not, for the reason above.

  AND THE CLASSIFICATION DOES NOT GENERALISE EITHER. The rule of
  plot_isolated_bursts_population.py -- retrigger / flash / beam-like / other,
  written down from the three run_000060 cases characterised by hand in section
  9 -- sorts the 340 as:

      beam-like   211  (62.1%)
      flash         0  ( 0.0%)
      retrigger     2  ( 0.6%)
      other       127  (37.4%)

  Over a third of the sample falls outside every category, and the two classes
  that the section-9 case studies were supposed to establish (a whole-slab flash
  and a chip retrigger) account for 2 cases out of 340. Three hand-picked
  acquisitions from one run were a sample of three, not a taxonomy. The "other"
  bin was left in the figure so that this would be visible rather than hidden,
  and it is.
