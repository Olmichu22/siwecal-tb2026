================================================================================
 SiW-ECAL TB2026 -- HOW A HIT BECOMES AN ENERGY
 The calibration procedure as it stands, and why each step is what it is.
 Last measured: 2026-07-14, on run_000012 (th230) and run_000072 (th220).
================================================================================

THE ONE-LINE VERSION
--------------------
  adc_high < 1500 :  energy = (adc_high - ped_hg) / MIP_hg
  adc_high >= 1500:  energy = ((adc_low - ped_lg - c) / k) / MIP_hg

  with  k = 0.0962,  c = 1.45,  and MIP_hg ALWAYS taken from the th220 tables,
  whatever threshold the run was actually taken at.

  MIP_lg is not used anywhere. That is the point of the whole thing.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. THE PEDESTAL  --  from the run's OWN threshold
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Measured per (slab, chip, channel, SCA) from the samples where the channel did NOT
fire. The trigger cannot bias it, because the trigger is what defines the samples
it is NOT measured from.

Each threshold keeps its own pedestal table, and that is deliberate. The th220 and
th230 tables genuinely differ -- median 1.27 ADC in the high gain, RMS 3.3, with
68% of channel-SCAs apart by more than 1 ADC -- and that is real run-to-run drift,
not error. Forcing one threshold's pedestals onto another run would inject an error
that does not exist today.

  VERIFIED: the low-gain baseline of non-triggering samples sits at -0.25 ADC
  relative to the table, i.e. at zero. The pedestal table is correct.
  (diagnostics/compare/pedestal_shift_*.png)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. THE MIP (high gain)  --  ALWAYS from th220, for EVERY run
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the step that looks wrong and is not. A run taken at th230 is calibrated
against th220's MIP table.

The reason: the MIP MPV, in ADC, is a property of the PREAMP, not of the
discriminator. run_000060 (DAC 220) and run_000004 (DAC 230) have identical
FeedbackCap (15), HoldDelay (110) and FSPeakTime (2) -- only ThresholdDAC differs --
so one MIP has to give the same ADC in both. It does not:

    th220   MIP_hg = 21.69 ADC     agrees with the reference tool to 1.6%
    th230   MIP_hg = 32.40 ADC     inflated x1.47

The MIP histogram is only filled when the channel FIRES, so the discriminator cuts
the spectrum from the LEFT. At th230 that cut lands at 18-22 ADC -- on top of the
MIP peak itself (~22) -- removing 70-83% of the peak-region entries and dragging the
fit upward. At th220 it cuts at 11-13, well below the peak, and the fit is clean.

  PROOF that the gains really are equal: normalise the two MIP spectra on their
  TAILS (ADC > 40), which no trigger reaches. They overlap (ratio 1.12; it would be
  1.49 if the gains differed). What does NOT overlap is the peak region -- exactly
  where th230's trigger ate the data.
  (diagnostics/compare/th220_vs_th230_slab11_split30.png)

  So th230's MIP table is not correct even for th230. th220's is correct for
  everyone. Code: siwecal_eventbuilder/cli.py::MIP_CALIB_TH = "220".
  Override with --mip-th <th> if you ever need the old behaviour.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. THE SWITCH  --  raw adc_high >= 1500
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Above this, the high-gain ADC no longer tracks the deposit and the energy is read
from the low gain instead.

1500 is measured, not guessed. Use the LOW gain as the ruler (it is linear over this
whole range), slice in adc_low, take the median adc_high per slice, and see where it
falls below the straight line:

    raw adc_high     1200     1500     1700     1900
    non-linearity    0.4%     1.2%     1.9%     3.9%     (both runs agree)

The switch used to be 1900, where the high gain is ALREADY 4% low -- a one-sided
under-read on every hit in the 1500-1900 band, and a bias does not average away over
the hits of an event the way noise does.

1900 was chosen to keep hits AWAY from the low gain, back when the low gain meant
MIP_lg (step 4). That reason is now gone. What moving the switch down costs instead
is noise -- low-gain noise (~1 ADC) is amplified by 1/k = 10.4 -- and the trade
crosses over right here:

    switch           1200     1500     1900
    bias  (syst.)    0.35%    1.15%    3.85%
    noise (random)   0.87%    0.69%    0.55%

  (diagnostics/th230/hg_linearity_000012_th230.png, and th220/ for run72)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. THE LOW GAIN  --  anchored to the high gain, NOT calibrated on its own MIP
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is the substantive change.

WHY THE OLD WAY IS BROKEN. Above the switch the old code did energy = (adc_low -
ped_lg) / MIP_lg. But MIP_lg cannot be measured:

    a low-gain MIP  = 2.08 ADC   (= k * MIP_hg)
    low-gain noise  = 0.92-1.59 ADC
    -> signal-to-noise = 1.4-2.8, and in 14-23% of channels the NOISE EXCEEDS A
       WHOLE MIP.

The Landau fit has nothing to grip. It lands on 3.16-4.46 ADC instead of 2.08 --
inflated 1.5x to 2x -- in EVERY ONE of the 7 muon runs, quiet ones included. Dividing
by an inflated MIP UNDER-READS the energy of every saturated hit.

WHAT WE DO INSTEAD. Anchor the low gain to the high gain, which IS well calibrated.
In the FIDUCIAL REGION -- adc_high - pedestal between 150 and 1200, which sits below
both the switch and the onset of non-linearity, so the bend cannot contaminate the
line meant to replace it -- the two branches trace out a straight line:

    adc_low - ped_lg  =  k * (adc_high - ped_hg)  +  c

Invert it to recover the high-gain ADC an unsaturated preamp would have given, and
divide by MIP_hg:

    adc_high_equiv = (adc_low - ped_lg - c) / k
    energy         = adc_high_equiv / MIP_hg

MIP_lg never appears. This is standard dual-gain intercalibration: the low gain's job
is to EXTEND THE DYNAMIC RANGE, not to be a second independent calibration.

HOW THE LINE IS FITTED. By the MEDIAN of adc_low in each slice of adc_high -- never
by least squares on the hits, which is dragged off the band by its tails and reports
c = +3.13 where the medians say +1.60. Both lines are drawn in the plot so you can
see the red one miss the data.

    parameter        run_000012 (th230)   run_000072 (th220)   USED
    k                     0.0961               0.0963          0.0962
    1/k                   10.41                10.39           10.4
    c  [ADC]              +1.60                +1.29           +1.45

  k is solid: two runs, two thresholds, agreeing to 0.2%.
  c is real but only good to ~0.5 ADC, and the error is SYSTEMATIC (it moves with
  the fit region), not the +/-0.03 a least-squares covariance would claim. It
  matters little: c shifts the energy by c/k ~ 15 high-gain ADC ~ 0.7 MIP against a
  switch at 1500 ADC ~ 58 MIP.

  (diagnostics/th220/gain_anchor_fit_000072_th220.png, and th230/ for run12)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. WHAT IT DID, AND WHAT IT DID NOT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
run_000012 (74 GeV electrons, th230), same 108,624 events throughout:

    calibration                                    mu        sigma/mu
    ------------------------------------------------------------------
    old: th230 MIP + MIP_lg switch at 1900        2157      0.343
    th220 MIP + MIP_lg switch at 1900             3065      0.341
    th220 MIP + ANCHORED low gain, switch 1500    3290      0.350

  THE ABSOLUTE SCALE MOVES +52%. Most of it (+42%) is dropping th230's inflated
  MIP_hg; the rest (+7%) is dropping MIP_lg on the saturated hits. Both were
  under-reads, so both push the same way.

  THE RESOLUTION DOES NOT IMPROVE. sigma/mu goes 0.343 -> 0.350, 2% WORSE. This was
  predicted to improve and it does not. The reason: MIP_lg's inflation is close to a
  common factor across channels, so it was a SCALE error, not a scatter source, and
  fixing a scale error moves the mean, not the width. Anchoring also raises the
  saturated hits ~50%, and those are the shower-core hits that fluctuate most, so
  sigma grows with them.

  YOU GAINED SCALE, NOT RESOLUTION. Scale was what was broken.

THE STRONGEST EVIDENCE THAT THE ANCHORING IS RIGHT is not the mean -- it is the
insensitivity. Moving the switch 1900 -> 1500:

    anchored energy:   mu 3285 -> 3290   (+0.16%)
    old MIP_lg energy: mu 3065 -> 2983   (-2.68%)

A correct intercalibration makes the two branches agree in their overlap, so it
should not matter where you cross between them. It doesn't -- 17x less than the old
one. A broken calibration makes your answer depend on an arbitrary tuning knob, and
that is exactly what MIP_lg did.

  (diagnostics/th230/energy_calib_000012.png -- panel 3 is the control: the 8,472
   events with no saturated hit are bit-for-bit identical, so nothing moved that
   should not have.)


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. WHAT IS STILL OPEN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
* NO ABSOLUTE REFERENCE YET. We know 3290 MIP is more correct than 2157 for 74 GeV.
  We do NOT know it is correct. The test is run_000072: same 74 GeV beam, taken at
  th220, whose MIP was never biased. If run12 and run72 now converge on the same mu,
  the fix is validated. Until then, quote the change, not the number.

* c DEPENDS ON THE RUN and we do not know why (+1.60 vs +1.29). The gap is the size
  of the systematic, so it may be nothing. Do not build on it.

* WHY THE LOW-GAIN BASELINE SHIFTS AT ALL. c is not an artefact -- the low-gain
  baseline really sits ~1-2 ADC high on a hit that fired (measured directly, with a
  control that comes out at 0.00). Plausibly crosstalk from the discriminator into
  the analogue branch, but that is a hypothesis, not a measurement.

* th210 IS BLOCKED. 13 of its 14 runs decode to zero acquisitions (the raw files are
  <run>.bin, not <run>_raw.bin, and the frame layout differs). Needs the format spec.

* hit_energy_nocalib is written alongside hit_energy on every event, holding the old
  MIP_lg value. They differ ONLY above the switch. Use it to reproduce any of the
  comparisons above without re-reconstructing.
